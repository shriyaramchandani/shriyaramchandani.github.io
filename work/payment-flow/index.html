Payment flow for dining

Helping diners understand their savings, one decision at a time.

The starting point

What our payments flow looked like

The user opens the restaurant page and taps Pay bill.

Several offers compete for attention. None can be applied on this page.

The main offer is automatically applied in the cart.

The other two types of offers appear in 2 separate rails.

Every offer was competing for attention.

District’s payment flow was designed to surface every possible offer. Diners entered their bill amount, then reached a cart where the restaurant discount was applied automatically and other offers appeared in separate rails.

The growing problem

A new offer meant another section in the cart.

We launched a loyalty program with cashback in the form of “DineCoins”. Adding DineCoins redemption meant another offer section in the cart.

We also introduced a split payment feature and placed it above all the offers. Diners now had to navigate split payment, DineCoins, exclusive offers, payment offers and a bill summary.

The attempt to show everything made it harder to focus on the offers that were actually applicable.

What the data showed

Most users who reached the cart were dropping off.

70%of repeat users dropped off from cart
77%of new users dropped off from cart

From calls with users, we found out:

  • When no offer was available, users could choose to pay directly at the restaurant.
  • Some users were checking potential savings before visiting a restaurant, without intending to pay yet.
  • Others interacted with the offer widgets but still left without paying, particularly new users.

This last set of users is who we needed to target: users who intended to pay but were still dropping off.

The hypothesis

In the attempt to surface all possible offers, the users were being overwhelmed and couldn’t figure out the savings

Prioritising what offers/information to show at which point and presenting all the offers in a streamlined manner would help the user understand what’s happening better

Setting the hierarchy

So, what should users see first?

To streamline the offers, we first needed to decide what deserved the most attention at each step of payment.

Restaurant offers apply automatically, while promos and bank offers need a user action. Diners can also redeem existing DineCoins and earn more for a future bill.

We used these differences to set the following priority order:

  1. 01

    Restaurant offer

    Auto applied

    Highest priority. Provides maximum value to the user.

  2. 02

    DineCoins earned

    Applies manually

    Medium priority. Earning DineCoins can provide a reason to pay through District even when no other discount is available.

  3. 03

    Additional offers

    Applies manually

    Lower priority. These include bank offers, which are applied in only 5% of our bill payments, and District promos, which are only sometimes available.

The redesigned amount-entry screen

One task, with a clearer reason to continue.

The restaurant offer sits with the bill input, followed by the DineCoins cashback message. Two additional offers, when available, are introduced more subtly above the payment actions.

  • Keep the main discount close to the amount being entered.
  • Explain the cashback earned after payment separately.
  • Move the split-bill action to this step to reduce confusion in the cart.

The redesigned cart

Bring relevant offers into one place.

The restaurant discount is already applied. The bill amount, savings and DineCoins earned are grouped at the top so diners can understand the value of paying through District.

Additional offers are stacked in a single widget. Diners can apply the surfaced offers or open the full list, instead of scanning multiple offer rails.
