Designing a digital menu experience to browse, order and pay from your device

Until a year ago, District helped people discover good restaurants, make reservations, and pay their bills with offers. But the journey between these steps had been neglected.

Meanwhile, more restaurants were switching to digital menus and ordering.

So we decided to take a stab at it.

Bean Brew & Beyond · Table 3

Current scenario

What typically happens between entering and exiting a restaurant?

  1. 01Get seated
  2. 02Browse the menu
  3. 03Ask the waiter for recommendations
  4. 04Place the order
  5. 05Food arrives
  6. 06Order more
  7. 07Ask for the bill
  8. 08Pay the bill

The points in this journey where District can help

Browsing the menu

Long menus make it hard to find a dish. Diners rely on staff for recommendations and details.

Placing an order

Guests wait for a waiter to come to their table and take the order. This can take longer during rush hours.

Asking for and paying the bill

Guests call waiter, waiter comes, then he goes back and comes back with the bill, then guests ask for a QR code, then he goes back... you get it

Points apart from this journey where District can help

Table assistance

Guests need staff for water, cutlery or a lighter. Getting their attention can be difficult during rush hours.

Wi-Fi connectivity

A poor network makes browsing the menu, placing an order and paying the bill slow and frustrating.

What should the experience enable?

  • Browse the menu and discover recommendations
  • Place and manage orders
  • Pay the bill
  • Request assistance at the table
  • Connect to the restaurant’s Wi-Fi

Information architecture

How should the experience be structured?

After deciding what features to build, it was time to figrue out what will be where. But we needed to answer these questions first

Should menu be the page they land on or should there be a storefront?

We decided to go with a storefront because we also wanted to show other things like information about the restaurants, promotional banners by the restaurant, if we made menu the first page, we would have to keep everything above the menu because menu will be very long, which means it would keep shifting to the bottom with every widget we added, so we decided to keep a storefront instead

Should table assistance features be kept on the storefront or the menu page?

they should be accessible from both places because user might be on any of the pages when they need it
